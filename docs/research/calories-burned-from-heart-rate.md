# Which heart-rate energy-expenditure formula fits Serenix, how accurate is it, and does stored data suffice for daily calories burned?

**Date:** 2026-11-04 · **Unblocks:** whether issue #53 ("Calories burned from heart rate and Load") can be specced, and what the Profile must gain first

## Answer
> **Since amended:** ADR 0008 (2026-11-04) keeps body weight out of the Profile: formulas use
> Trend Weight (or the latest Weigh-in). Only height is added to the Profile.

- **Recommend a two-part model:** resting/sedentary energy from Mifflin-St Jeor BMR, plus active energy from Keytel et al. (2005) applied per minute only when HR is clearly above rest (an HR-flex threshold, e.g. Resting HR + a margin or HR Zone 1 and up). This is the published "flex-HR" structure; Keytel alone badly overestimates at low HR.
- **Expect roughly ±10–20% error on a day total for an individual, and worse per workout** (Keytel's group-level r=0.857 without VO2max, 0.913 with it, hides large individual errors). Present it as an estimate, never as a precise number.
- **Stored data does not suffice.** Serenix has no body weight (and no height); every formula needs weight, and Mifflin-St Jeor needs height too. Sex and age (birth year) exist, but sex can be "prefer not to say".
- **Per-minute HR is only kept 30 days** (History Records); a Day keeps only HR Zone seconds, Load, Resting HR and Max HR used. So calories must be computed and stored per Day when the Day is worked out (like Load), or it can't be backfilled past 30 days.
- Before a spec: add weight (kg) and height (cm) to the Profile, decide the fallback for "prefer not to say", and store a per-Day calories value (resting + active).

## Findings
- **Keytel et al. 2005** ([J Sports Sci 23(3):289–297, doi:10.1080/02640410470001730089](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640410470001730089)): mixed-effects model on 115 regularly active adults (18–45 y) in steady-state exercise. Without VO2max: EE (kJ/min) = −55.0969 + 0.6309·HR + 0.1988·weight(kg) + 0.2017·age for men, and −20.4022 + 0.4472·HR − 0.1263·weight + 0.074·age for women (coefficients as widely reproduced; **not verified against the paper**, which is paywalled). Abstract (verified via Europe PMC): 115 regularly exercising adults, 18–45 y, 47–120 kg, steady-state stages at 35/62/80% VO2max; r=0.913 (R² 83.3%) with VO2max, r=0.857 (R² 73.4%) without. Derived from exercise, not rest; it is not valid near resting HR (it yields non-physiological values there), which is why it must be gated by a threshold.
- **HR-flex method** (Spurr et al. 1988, [Am J Clin Nutr 48(3):552–559, doi:10.1093/ajcn/48.3.552](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/48.3.552); Ceesay et al. 1989, [Br J Nutr 61(2):175–186, doi:10.1079/BJN19890107](https://doi.org/10.1079/BJN19890107)): below a "flex HR" (between the highest resting and lowest exercise HR) EE is taken as resting EE; above it, from an individual HR–VO2 calibration. Spurr (22 individually calibrated subjects vs calorimetry): no significant group difference in daily EE; individual daily EE errors from −15% to +20%. Ceesay (20 subjects, whole-body calorimetry): mean −1.2% (SD 6.2%), individual range −11.4% to +10.6%. Both used individual HR–VO2 calibration, which Serenix would not have, so its errors will be larger. Review: Leonard 2003 ([Am J Hum Biol 15(4):479–489, doi:10.1002/ajhb.10187](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajhb.10187)) — flex-HR is a standard tool for free-living daily EE.
- **Mifflin-St Jeor 1990** ([Am J Clin Nutr 51(2):241–247, doi:10.1093/ajcn/51.2.241](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/51.2.241)): 498 healthy adults (247 women, 251 men), 19–78 y, normal-weight and obese. Full regression: REE (kcal/day) = 9.99·weight(kg) + 6.25·height(cm) − 4.92·age(y) + 166·sex(male=1, female=0) − 161, R²=0.71; the paper's simplified forms, which it says lose no predictive value: 10·weight + 6.25·height − 5·age + 5 (men), − 161 (women). Frankenfield et al. 2005 systematic review ([J Am Diet Assoc 105(5):775–789, doi:10.1016/j.jada.2005.02.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jada.2005.02.005)) found it the most reliable of four common equations, within 10% of measured RMR in more non-obese and obese people than any other (the ~82% / ~70% shares are from my memory of the full text, not verified).
- **Consumer wearables** (Shcherbina et al. 2017, [J Pers Med 7(2):3, doi:10.3390/jpm7020003](https://doi.org/10.3390/jpm7020003)): 7 wrist devices, 60 volunteers: no device achieved EE error below 20%, while most had median HR error under 5%. Sets a realistic expectation bar.
- **Profile has no weight or height**: fields are name, birthYear, sex, maxHR only — `src/SerenixCore/Profile.swift:15-23`. Sex is female / male / preferNotToSay — `src/SerenixCore/Profile.swift:4-8`. Searching `src` and `specs` for "weight"/"calor" finds only font weights.
- **Per-Day storage**: `DayLoad` keeps load, missingReason, heartRate (HR Zone seconds total and hourly, Max HR used) and restingHR — `src/SerenixCore/Day.swift:166-185`, `src/SerenixCore/DayHeartRate.swift:6-14`. No per-minute HR is kept on the Day; HR Zone seconds are too coarse to apply Keytel per minute (a zone spans 10% of Max HR ≈ 19 bpm ≈ 12 kJ/min at the male slope).
- **Where to compute**: Load is worked out from the Day's History Records with Profile — `src/SerenixCore/Load.swift:31-52`; calories would follow the same path (1 Hz HR → per-minute mean → gated Keytel + BMR/1440 per minute). Raw History Records are pruned after 30 days (docs/architecture.md, Data model).
- **Specs**: Load is spec 005 (`specs/005-load.md`) and its popup with HR Zones is spec 006 (`specs/006-load-popup.md`); neither mentions energy or calories.

## Open / uncertain
- **Verified (2026-11-04, abstracts via Europe PMC / search):** Mifflin-St Jeor coefficients (both forms), cohort and R²; Keytel cohort and r values; Spurr, Ceesay and Shcherbina accuracy figures; Frankenfield's "most reliable" conclusion; all DOIs (Leonard corrected to doi:10.1002/ajhb.10187).
- **Not verified:** Keytel's equation coefficients (full text paywalled at Taylor & Francis; only secondary calculators reproduce them, and they agree on the male HR slope 0.6309 and female 0.4472). Get the paper (e.g. via a library) and confirm every coefficient before committing test literals. Frankenfield's ~82%/~70% shares also unverified.
- Fallback for sex "prefer not to say": average the male and female equations, or require sex for calories.
- Gate threshold choice (Resting HR + N bpm vs HR Zone 1 at 50% Max HR) has no single published value; flex HR is individually calibrated in the literature.
- Keytel was derived on 18–45 y active adults; validity outside that range is untested.
- Whether to include sleep at BMR (Mifflin) or slightly below (sleeping metabolic rate is ~5–10% lower) is a product choice.
- Interaction with HealthKit export (active vs basal energy are separate types) needs deciding in the spec.
