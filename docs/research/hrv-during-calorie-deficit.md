# How do HRV and Sleeping HR change during a calorie deficit, low energy availability or overreaching, how many Nights show a real shift, and what supports diet breaks?

**Date:** 2026-11-04 · **Unblocks:** Deficit Check trigger rules (when to suggest a diet break or slower pace) — issue #55

## Answer
- Judge the trend, not one Night. Compare a **7-Night rolling mean of ln(RMSSD)** with the 14-Night Baseline. Count it as a meaningful shift only when it falls outside **Baseline ± 0.5 × Spread** (the smallest worthwhile change, SWC). Use at least 5 of the 7 Nights.
- Candidate trigger for the Deficit Check: while the user reports a deficit, the 7-Night HRV mean sits **below** the SWC band **and** the 7-Night Sleeping HR mean sits **above** Baseline by more than 0.5 × Spread, for **≥ 3 Nights in a row**. Then suggest a slower pace or a 1–2 week diet break at maintenance.
- Second trigger (watch for it, but word it softly): HRV rises **above** the band while Sleeping HR **drops** well below Baseline (for example by ≥ 5 bpm over 7 Nights) during a long deficit. Long energy restriction and functional overreaching can both cause bradycardia and parasympathetic "saturation". So falling HR or rising HRV is not proof of good recovery. Pair this pattern with symptoms (fatigue, poor sleep, missed periods) before suggesting a break.
- Do not trigger on a single Night, or until 14 Nights of Baseline exist (Readiness stops Calibrating after 4 Nights, but the HRV Baseline uses 14), or after alcohol, illness or travel. Keep the wording "consider", never a diagnosis. RED-S needs a clinician, and HR/HRV alone can't diagnose it (IOC 2023).
- Diet breaks are a reasonable thing to offer, not something proven to help. MATADOR (2 weeks on, 2 weeks at maintenance) gave more fat loss per week of dieting. Trials in lean, trained people show little or no extra benefit for body composition. So frame it as a choice for wellbeing and sticking with the diet.

## Findings
- **RED-S definition and signs.** The 2023 IOC consensus defines RED-S as impaired physiological function caused by problematic low energy availability. Bradycardia/low resting HR and hypotension are listed as cardiovascular signs. HR markers alone can't make a diagnosis, which needs a clinical assessment tool (IOC REDs CAT2) ([Mountjoy et al., Br J Sports Med 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994)).
- **Long energy restriction lowers HR.** The Minnesota Starvation Experiment saw marked bradycardia under semi-starvation (Keys et al., *The Biology of Human Starvation*, 1950). Anorexia nervosa shows low HR and, in most of the 20 papers reviewed, parasympathetic dominance. The findings were mixed, and the authors judge HRV a research tool, not a routine assessment ([Mazurak et al., Eur Eat Disord Rev 2011](https://doi.org/10.1002/erv.1081)). These are extreme cases. Moderate dieting gives a smaller fall in HR that tracks the drop in energy intake and thyroid/leptin signals (adaptive thermogenesis; see MATADOR below).
- **Overreaching can push HRV either way.** A meta-analysis found that HRV and resting HR shifts with training status are small and inconsistent, and that functional overreaching often shows *higher* vagal markers ([Bellenger et al., Sports Med 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-016-0484-2)). Functionally overreached triathletes showed parasympathetic hyperactivity: lower HR and higher HRV ([Le Meur et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc 2013](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e3182980125)).
- **Use rolling means of ln(RMSSD).** The 7-day rolling mean and its coefficient of variation track adaptation and nonfunctional overreaching better than single days. A single day is too noisy ([Plews et al., Eur J Appl Physiol 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-012-2354-4); [Plews et al., Sports Med 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-013-0071-8)).
- **How many Nights.** A 7-day mean needs at least 3 randomly spread valid measurements in the week to match the full 7-day value ([Plews et al., Int J Sports Physiol Perform 2014](https://doi.org/10.1123/IJSPP.2013-0455)). So the earliest meaningful shift is about 1 week. Persistent changes over 2+ weeks are more convincing.
- **SWC around 0.5 × SD.** Buchheit reviews HR-based monitoring. He suggests reading a change against the smallest worthwhile change (about 0.5 × the athlete's between-day SD of ln(RMSSD)). He also says HRV changes have to be read alongside HR and context: falling HRV with rising HR suggests strain, and rising HRV with falling HR can be saturation ([Buchheit, Front Physiol 2014](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2014.00073)).
- **MATADOR.** Obese men were assigned to 16 weeks of continuous restriction, or 2 weeks restriction alternating with 2 weeks at energy balance. The intermittent group lost more fat and kept more of it off at 6 months, with less adaptive thermogenesis ([Byrne et al., Int J Obes 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/ijo.2017.206)).
- **Diet breaks in trained or lean people.** The ICECAP trial alternated 3-week blocks of moderate restriction with 1-week breaks at maintenance for resistance-trained adults over 12 weeks of dieting. Fat mass and fat-free mass ended up similar to continuous dieting, with reduced appetite ([Peos et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc 2021](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000002636)). A secondary analysis found that the breaks improved leg muscle endurance and lowered hunger and irritability ([Peos et al., PLoS ONE 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33587549/)). In a 7-week trial, 2-day carbohydrate refeeds were reported to keep more fat-free mass and resting metabolic rate ([Campbell et al., J Funct Morphol Kinesiol 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/jfmk5010019)). A published reanalysis of the shared data found only dry fat-free mass differed, and fat loss did not differ.

## Open / uncertain
- Few or no studies measure **nocturnal** RMSSD from a wrist wearable through a moderate (for example 20–25%) deficit in non-athletes. The direction for ordinary dieters (HRV up or down) isn't established, so the "HRV up, HR down" rule is speculative.
- The SWC multiplier (0.5) and the 3-Night streak are conventions from athlete monitoring, not values tested for diet-break decisions. They should be tuned on real user data.
- Verified 2026-11-04 by web search: the DOIs and main claims for Plews 2014, Mazurak (online 2010, issue 2011), Peos 2021 (MSSE and PLoS ONE) and Campbell 2020 (published in JFMK, not Int J Exerc Sci as first written). The Peos MSSE DOI resolves, but its full text was not read. Not re-fetched this pass: IOC 2023, Bellenger 2016, Le Meur 2013, Plews 2012/2013, Buchheit 2014 and Byrne 2018. Their DOIs and claims come from prior knowledge. Keys 1950 is a book with no link.
- Serenix doesn't know whether the user is in a deficit. The guard needs the user to say so (or log intake), or it can't tell diet effects apart from training, illness or alcohol.
- The 14-Night Baseline drifts with a sustained shift. After about 2 weeks a long-running deficit looks "normal". Consider freezing a pre-diet Baseline.
